<p>The linguist Paul Grice examined the structure of effective conversations. His work [1] gives us four principles, or maxims, to observe at all times:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>The maxim of quantity</strong>, where one tries to be as informative as one possibly can, and gives as much information as is needed, and no more.</li>
<li><strong>The maxim of quality</strong>, where one tries to be truthful, and does not give information that is false or that is not supported by evidence.</li>
<li><strong>The maxim of relation</strong>, where one tries to be relevant, and says things that are pertinent to the discussion.</li>
<li><strong>The maxim of manner</strong>, when one tries to be as clear, as brief, and as orderly as one can in what one says, and where one avoids obscurity and ambiguity.</li>
</ul>
<h3>To help with this, a few short heuristics:</h3>
<ul>
<li>
<p><strong>Always look for and cite multiple sources.</strong> Either link them or put them in an understandable section at the bottom.</p>
</li>
<li>
<p><strong>Aim for a clear, direct and conversational tone. Be blunt.</strong> Swearing is fine, as long as it serves a clear purpose of conveying some useful information. We’re not here to be polite: we’re here to be right.</p>
</li>
<li>
<p><strong>Active voice, not passive.</strong> “...was stated by the CEB”. “...treasury was depleted by the banker.” These are all far weaker than: “The CEB stated that” “The banker depleted the treasury.”</p>
</li>
<li>
<p><strong>Explain jargon and acronyms; explain institutions, relations, and authority.</strong> Every field has specific operational language that makes sense to practitioners within a field. However, we cannot expect general readers to know this jargon. What is the PUCSL? What is a regulator? Even when it lengthens an article, take the time to pen a concise explanation when introducing a term.</p>
</li>
<li>
<p><strong>When editing, aim for a grade 8-9 readability score on the</strong> <strong><a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flesch%E2%80%93Kincaid_readability_tests">Flesch-Kincaid scale</a>****.</strong> Reader’s Digest operates at a grade 8-9 level; Time Magazine is slightly lower. Hemingway’s <em>The Old Man and the Sea</em> is at a grade 4 level. Essentially, nobody should need a college degree to read our work.</p>
</li>
<li>
<p><strong>Use commas. Use the Oxford comma.</strong> This is what happens when you don’t:</p>
<p><img loading="lazy" src="/images/general/use-commas.png" alt=""></p>
</li>
<li>
<p><strong>Name first, designation second</strong> Mahinda Rajapaksa, Prime Minister of Sri Lanka.</p>
</li>
<li>
<p><strong>On numbers:</strong> 0-9: use letters. If you insist on using numerals, make sure it’s a double digit: 01, 02, 03. Else, say one two three. 10 and upwards: use numerals.</p>
</li>
<li>
<p><strong>Dates:</strong> Date - Month - Year 01 January 2022 — not 1st January</p>
</li>
</ul>
